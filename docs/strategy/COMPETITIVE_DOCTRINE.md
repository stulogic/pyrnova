# Pyrnova competitive doctrine

_Status: canonical strategic authority · effective 2026-09-09 · informed by the 2026-09-09 red team
review and strategic data research (`docs/research/`)_

> **Authority level.** Binding strategic authority. It sits with `01-PROJECT-AUTHORITY.md` and
> `docs/strategy/PHASE_1_PRODUCT_AUTHORITY.md` as current strategic authority (`00-INDEX.md` hierarchy,
> level 1). It governs *why* and *against whom* Pyrnova builds; it does **not** authorize implementation
> (`02-EXECUTION.md` alone does) and never overrides the finishability rule (D-047) or Phase 1 non-goals
> (D-048). See D-052. It is a consistent companion to the overarching
> `docs/strategy/PRODUCT_COMMERCIAL_AUTHORITY.md` (owner-approved) and defers to it on any direct conflict;
> the canonical AUGMENT → ATTENTION → WORKFLOW → BUDGET → SYSTEM-OF-INTELLIGENCE displacement sequence and
> the commercial model are stated there.

Pyrnova is not being built to participate politely in an established intelligence-software category. It
is being built to take customers, budget, attention, and strategic relevance from incumbent products.
Competitors are adversaries in the market, not product-design partners. Study them aggressively; do not
imitate them for parity's sake. The objective is **material superiority**, not comparability.

## Competitive objective (the Phase 1 commercial test)

The Phase 1 commercial test is **not** "Can Pyrnova also do procurement intelligence?" It is:

> **Can Pyrnova repeatedly tell an existing GovWin, GovTribe, or equivalent customer something materially
> important that their existing workflow did not — early enough and clearly enough to change what they
> do?**

If yes, press the advantage. If no, determine why — do not rationalize weak differentiation. This is the
falsifiable commercial validation Phase 1 requires (red team review; D-045).

## Domination standard

For strategically important workflows, seek one of three positions — "comparable" is not an attractive
destination:

1. **CLEARLY BETTER** — the same important job, done materially better.
2. **UNIQUELY CAPABLE** — answers an economically important question competitors do not answer
   adequately.
3. **STRUCTURALLY ADVANTAGED** — historical intelligence, customer context, consequence graph, outcome
   learning, or accumulated proprietary state creates an advantage that becomes progressively harder to
   reproduce.

## Assume imitation — build advantages that survive it

Assume successful Pyrnova features will be copied. Therefore interfaces, AI features, prompts,
dashboards, alerts, natural-language search, and graph visualization are **temporary** advantages, not
defenses (consistent with the non-moats in D-049).

Prioritize advantages that survive imitation: accumulated historical state; outcome calibration;
rejection history; customer-specific exposure models; rights-safe proprietary relationships; evidence
lineage; point-in-time intelligence; cross-domain precursor performance; customer-contributed context;
and workflow knowledge generated through repeated use.

Every major product decision must ask: **if an incumbent copied the visible feature in six months, what
would Pyrnova still possess that they would not?**

## Attack weakness, not strength

Do not spend resources fighting an incumbent where its structural advantage is overwhelming. Do **not**
try to beat:

| Incumbent | Overwhelming structural strength — do not attack head-on |
|---|---|
| Bloomberg | market & financial data breadth |
| AlphaSense | licensed research-corpus breadth |
| Dataminr | global raw event detection |
| Kpler | proprietary maritime / commodity intelligence |
| Altana, Exiger | proprietary supply-chain visibility |
| PitchBook | private-capital datasets |

Use such information where economically justified (rent evidence you cannot recreate — strategic data
research; D-008). Attack the **adjacent problem they solve poorly.** Pyrnova's battlefield:

> What changed externally, how does it specifically affect **this** organization, what commercial
> consequence follows, how certain are we, what evidence proves it, and what happened afterward?

Own that problem.

## No competitive deference

Do not reject an idea merely because an incumbent offers something superficially similar. Existing
competition proves customers spend money; it does not prove the problem is solved well. Ask instead: Is
their implementation actually good? Does it solve the same customer decision? Is the information timely
and customer-specific? Does it explain consequences, preserve historical state, and learn from outcomes?
Is it priced rationally? **Can Pyrnova collapse several incumbent workflows into one better decision
system?**

## Customer displacement (evidence standard)

Prefer design customers who **already use** an incumbent (GovWin, GovTribe, AlphaSense, or another
relevant product). A pilot alongside an incumbent is stronger evidence than a customer with no existing
intelligence workflow. The ultimate commercial evidence is not praise — it is **displaced spend.**

Measure per pilot: intelligence Pyrnova found first; intelligence the incumbent missed; intelligence
both found; relative lead time; false positives; actions triggered; decisions changed; analyst time
saved; incumbent workflows abandoned or reduced; and willingness to reallocate existing software budget
to Pyrnova.

## Aggressive product standard

Do not ship mediocrity to satisfy a checklist. Do not preserve a weak workflow because engineering
effort was already spent (sunk cost is not product value). When Pyrnova finds a substantially better way
to perform a core customer job: verify it, preserve architectural integrity, replace the inferior
approach, document the decision, and continue. **Scope control (D-042/D-047) prevents uncontrolled
expansion; it does not protect weak product decisions from competition.**

## Speed

Move faster than organizations burdened by large installed bases, legacy architecture, procurement
cycles, internal politics, multiple product lines, backward compatibility, and enterprise bureaucracy.
Pyrnova's small size is a competitive weapon. **Governance exists to preserve direction and speed, not
to become process theatre** — do not recreate incumbent bureaucracy inside the repository.

## Internal attitude

Assume: incumbents will defend valuable accounts; successful features will be copied; customers owe
Pyrnova nothing; technical elegance does not create market power; being interesting or approximately as
good is insufficient; intelligence that does not alter decisions has limited value; every unnecessary
manual step is an opening for a competitor; every false high-confidence conclusion spends customer
trust; every customer workflow Pyrnova genuinely eliminates is territory gained. The standard is not
hostility — it is **relentless competitive superiority.**

## Ethical and legal boundary (binding)

Competitive aggression does **not** authorize unlawful access, misrepresentation, theft of confidential
information, interference with competitor systems, misuse of customer information, violation of licenses
or contracts, or deceptive commercial practices. Pyrnova competes by building a superior product, moving
and learning faster, and exploiting legitimate weaknesses in incumbent offerings. Aggressive does not
mean careless. This boundary reinforces the licensing/rights and terms-of-use rules in
`01-PROJECT-AUTHORITY.md` and `docs/specs/SOURCE_INGESTION.md`.

## Governing question (apply to every major Phase 1 capability)

> **Why would a rational customer already paying for the incumbent switch budget, workflow, or attention
> to Pyrnova?**

If the answer is weak, the feature is **not finished**. If it is compelling, make the advantage
stronger. If the advantage compounds through data, outcomes, or customer usage, protect it. The
objective is not to join the intelligence-software market — it is to become the system customers
increasingly prefer over the products currently occupying it.
