> **Authority level.** Binding doctrine governing Pyrnova's external presentation, brand character, and
> disclosure posture (level 1 in `00-INDEX.md`, alongside the competitive doctrines). It shapes *how
> Pyrnova presents and discloses*; it does **not** authorize implementation (`02-EXECUTION.md` alone

## Two deliberate faces

Pyrnova presents one character to customers and a substantially harder character to competitors and the
market. The contrast is intentional: **a customer should conclude "I am glad these people are working for
us"; a competitor observing the same company should conclude "I need to understand what these people are
doing."**

### To customers

Exceptionally professional, discreet, calm, precise, evidence-led, reliable, controlled, commercially
serious, intellectually rigorous, and trustworthy under pressure. The impression: *these people know
things early, understand consequences, maintain discretion, and take our interests seriously.* **Never**
theatrical, juvenile, threatening toward customers, or edgy for branding effect. Competence carries the
threat implicitly. This is continuous with the **Product Language Authority (D-041)** — formal, precise,
operational voice; copy quality is part of acceptance.

### Internally / competitively

Adversarial, secretive by default, highly competitive, territorially protective, strategically selfish,
difficult to read, aggressive about lawful information acquisition, obsessive about competitors, hostile
to unnecessary dependency, intolerant of complacency, focused on winning rather than participating.
Competitors compete for the same customers, budgets, data, talent, attention, contracts, and strategic
position — study them accordingly (`COMPETITIVE_INTELLIGENCE_AND_DATA_AUTONOMY.md`).

## Rules of engagement

**Play hard. Exploit every legitimate advantage. Stay inside applicable law and contractual
obligations.** Do not adopt restrictions merely because they make competitors comfortable; do not confuse
politeness with ethics or incumbent convention with obligation; do not avoid an aggressive commercial
tactic merely because a competitor dislikes it. Where the permissible boundary is uncertain, **escalate
for review rather than automatically retreating** (the YELLOW rule, D-053). The objective is maximum
*legitimate* competitive freedom. The lawful/ethical boundaries in `COMPETITIVE_DOCTRINE.md` and
`COMPETITIVE_INTELLIGENCE_AND_DATA_AUTONOMY.md` remain absolute.

## Secrecy — default NEED TO KNOW (external disclosure)

Disclose what customers, counterparties, regulators, and law legitimately need to know. Do **not**
disclose internal capability, strategy, research, competitive intelligence, technical methods, source
economics, or future direction merely for openness's sake. Information asymmetry is valuable; competitors
should find Pyrnova difficult to study. Protect: roadmap; proprietary datasets; intelligence
methodologies; source strategies; competitive dossiers; customer intelligence; internal benchmarks;
inference methods; unreleased functionality; commercial experiments; pricing strategy; acquisition
targets; strategic weaknesses; research priorities. Do not advertise a capability before doing so creates
commercial advantage.

> **Scope of secrecy (clarification).** This governs *external* disclosure. It does **not** weaken
> Pyrnova's internal record-keeping discipline: the repository remains the full, detailed, durable source
> of truth (`00-INDEX.md`), and agents must keep authority/decisions/state complete and honest. Who may
> access the repository and other internal records is an owner-controlled decision, not a license to
> under-document internally. "Unknown stays unknown" and "do not fabricate" still bind.

## Visual identity authority

### Reference-board boundary

The owner-supplied branding board is **inspiration and north-star visual direction**, not literal final
artwork. It is not authority for slogans, taglines, filler or descriptive copy, card photography, product
imagery, or exact icon geometry. Do not reproduce those elements merely because they appear on the board.
All words on a designed surface remain subject to D-041 and the current product/commercial authorities.

### Brand and product hierarchy

**PYRNOVA is the institution and parent brand.** Scout, Strike, Vector and Atlas are a coordinated family
of customer-facing products. PyrAI is a shared intelligence capability/layer, visually subordinate to the
parent brand, **not a fifth peer product**. This visual hierarchy reflects, and does not redefine, the
locked platform/product boundaries in `PLATFORM_PRODUCT_ARCHITECTURE.md` (D-067/D-068).

### Color system — semantic assignments LOCKED

- **Pyrnova parent/platform:** black or near-black foundations; metallic white/silver for primary marks,
  type and structure; **cyan is the dominant highlight and interaction accent whenever no
  product-specific semantic color applies**.
- **Strike:** red/orange.
- **Scout:** green.
- **Vector:** blue.
- **Atlas:** violet/purple.
- **PyrAI:** blue-to-violet gradient treatment, contained within the wider cyan-led Pyrnova system and
  never presented with the visual weight of a fifth product identity.

Cyan is the visual spine for parent-brand emphasis, active states, selected navigation, focus treatments,
key interface emphasis, non-product data accents, subtle glows, links, dividers and general illumination.
Product colors are semantic and contextual: they may identify a product or product-owned meaning, but
must not replace cyan as the default platform accent. "Dominant" describes hierarchy, not surface area;
cyan should remain restrained and purposeful rather than becoming neon decoration.

The repository's accepted Pyrnova mark and published website work already use **`#25CFE8`** as canonical
brand cyan (`docs/brand/notion/pyrnova-notion-header.svg` and `docs/operations/WEBSITE_GO_EVIDENCE.md`).
Retain that value where an exact cyan token is required. This decision does **not** invent or freeze exact
red/orange, green, blue, violet/purple, metallic or gradient values. Production palette/token work may
define those values later without changing the semantic assignments above.

### Typography hierarchy — font classes LOCKED, exact families OPEN

- Use a **futuristic geometric display face** for the Pyrnova/product names and major headings.
- Use **wide-tracked uppercase supporting type** for labels, navigation, eyebrow text, metadata and brand
  support copy.
- Use a **clean, highly legible modern sans** for body and UI copy.

Font selection must preserve a premium technical character while keeping body text, controls and dense
operational information readable across sizes and accessibility settings. Do not hard-code a new font
family as brand authority without separate approval. Future asset/UI work should select within these
classes, document substitutions and keep the hierarchy intact.

### Form, imagery and family coherence

Use dark cinematic backgrounds, very high contrast, generous negative space, restrained luminous accents,
atmospheric depth and a premium technical, aerospace-adjacent visual language. The system should feel like
one family across Pyrnova and its products. Avoid indiscriminate neon, rainbow treatment, generic AI
glows, generic corporate technology imagery and decorative complexity that reduces operational clarity.

A shared geometric icon family for Strike, Scout, Vector and Atlas is desirable. Exact product marks and
icon geometry remain **OPEN until separately owner-approved**; the concept-board marks do not become
canonical by reference. Product imagery is also **UNFROZEN**. Select it to express the product's actual
role and evidence-backed function, not generic AI/corporate themes.

### Asset and UI generation rule

Before generating or approving a Pyrnova asset or UI, consult this section, D-041, and
`PLATFORM_PRODUCT_ARCHITECTURE.md`. Preserve the hierarchy, semantic color assignments, typography roles
and reference-board boundary. A concept may explore exact fonts, secondary tokens, product marks and
role-specific imagery, but must label them as proposals until separately approved. Do not present a
concept-board slogan, image, icon, token or font choice as repository authority.

## Visual & environmental character (non-customer-facing)

Non-customer-facing presentation may communicate authority, restraint, secrecy, technological
sophistication, institutional confidence, controlled menace, permanence, and capability. **Avoid**
costume-villain aesthetics, skulls, tactical clichés, faux-military branding, and adolescent aggression.
The reference is *an organization with substantial capability that does not feel the need to explain
itself* — dark, minimal, expensive without ostentation, precise, architectural, low-information, slightly
intimidating, deliberate. The intended impression: *there is substantially more happening here than they
are showing us.*

## Customer environment

Customer-facing environments keep the same underlying authority but **remove overt menace**. They
communicate **discretion + intelligence + competence + control** — not aggression or intimidation. Any
customer-facing surface Pyrnova builds inherits both this and D-041.

## Competitive reputation (earned through execution, not misconduct)

Seek a reputation among competitors for: appearing unexpectedly in important accounts; knowing markets
unusually well; detecting change quickly; understanding competitor products in detail; identifying
weaknesses accurately; moving into gaps before incumbents respond; replicating non-exclusive advantages
rather than depending on them; building proprietary replacements for strategically important rented
capabilities; exceptionally strong institutional memory; learning from every commercial encounter; and
rarely revealing its complete strategy. **Competitors should not fear misconduct — they should fear
execution.**

## Talent culture

Value discretion, intellectual aggression, curiosity, skepticism, competitive instinct, independent
thinking, speed, ownership, resourcefulness, and strategic awareness. Actively look for competitor
weakness, exploitable market structure, unnecessary vendor dependency, information asymmetry, customer
dissatisfaction, overlooked public evidence, operational inefficiency, and incumbent complacency — while
preserving Pyrnova's legal, contractual, and information-security boundaries. **Aggression without
discipline is liability; aggression with discipline is advantage.**

## Corporate self-interest

Pyrnova acts unapologetically in its own legitimate commercial interest. When interests conflict,
management seeks the outcome that best strengthens Pyrnova's customers, ownership, capabilities, autonomy,
information advantage, market position, margins, and enterprise value. Competitors defend their own
interests; Pyrnova advances its own.

## The standard

Pyrnova should never appear *desperate* to be dangerous; it should simply become capable enough that the
conclusion is unavoidable. Externally: **consummate professionals.** Internally: **relentlessly
competitive.** Strategically: **difficult to understand, harder to copy, unpleasant to compete against.**
Customers should want Pyrnova in their corner; competitors should wish it had chosen another market.
