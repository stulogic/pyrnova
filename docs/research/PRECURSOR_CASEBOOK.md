# Retrospective Precursor Casebook

**Purpose:** demonstrate, on the public record, that genuinely *upstream* events (statute / authorization
/ executive policy) precede procurement by a trackable lead time — the differentiation recompete-expiry
alone cannot provide. This is **retrospective methodology proof**, not a claim that Pyrnova predicted
anything. Each case uses a strict information cutoff and separates *genuinely early* signal from
*hindsight-obvious*.

> Honesty guardrail: written from public knowledge (assistant cutoff Jan 2026), not live-verified in
> this environment. Exact notice IDs, dollar figures, and dates below are approximate and **must be
> verified against primary sources before any customer-facing use.** Confidence tags reflect that.

Structure per case: **Chain · Cutoff · Knowable then · Hindsight-only · Pyrnova inference · Falsifiers at
the time · Observable outcome · Lead time · Beachhead relevance.**

---

## Case 1 — CHIPS and Science Act → NIST CHIPS Program Office → fab funding solicitations
- **Chain:** statute (CHIPS & Science Act, ~$52B) → Commerce/NIST stands up CHIPS Program Office →
  RFIs / strategy → Notice of Funding Opportunity for commercial fabrication → preliminary awards.
- **Cutoff:** day of signing, **Aug 9, 2022**.
- **Knowable then (high conf):** the money and the implementing department (Commerce/NIST) were named in
  the statute; a funding-opportunity process for semiconductor manufacturing was effectively certain.
- **Hindsight-only:** the exact NOFO date (first commercial-fab NOFO ~**Feb 2023**), award recipients,
  and amounts.
- **Pyrnova inference at cutoff:** "Commerce/NIST will issue microelectronics funding opportunities and
  supporting-services demand within ~6–12 months; watch NIST RFIs as the pre-solicitation tell." A
  contractor in microelectronics / test / systems engineering could position months ahead.
- **Falsifiers at the time:** appropriations not following authorization; program-office standup delay;
  legal/eligibility disputes.
- **Observable outcome:** CHIPS Program Office + RFIs (late 2022) → NOFO (early 2023) → awards (2024+).
- **Lead time:** statute → first NOFO ≈ **6–7 months**; the RFI tell led the NOFO by months again.
- **Beachhead relevance (med):** microelectronics/defense-industrial-base adjacency; strong general
  proof of the statute→solicitation mechanism.

## Case 2 — IIJA → NTIA BEAD broadband program → NOFO → state allocations → sub-procurement
- **Chain:** statute (Infrastructure Investment & Jobs Act, **Nov 15, 2021**; ~$42.45B BEAD) → NTIA
  program design → **BEAD NOFO (~May 2022)** → **state allocations (~Jun 2023)** → state broadband
  offices procure mapping/planning/build services.
- **Cutoff:** **Nov 15, 2021**.
- **Knowable then (high conf):** program named, funded, and assigned to NTIA; a state-by-state
  procurement wave was structurally implied.
- **Hindsight-only:** exact allocation formula outcomes, per-state dollar figures, NOFO date specifics.
- **Pyrnova inference at cutoff:** "NTIA issues program guidance within ~6–9 months; states stand up
  broadband offices → consulting, GIS/mapping, and build-out procurement demand follows." Positioning
  window of many months for services firms.
- **Falsifiers at the time:** rulemaking/formula disputes; state capacity delays; CR-driven timing slips.
- **Observable outcome:** NOFO 2022 → allocations mid-2023 → state solicitations 2023–2024.
- **Lead time:** statute → NOFO ≈ **6 months**; statute → state procurement wave ≈ **18–24 months**
  (long, high-value positioning runway).
- **Beachhead relevance (low–med):** civilian/state-capital, not defense — included as a clean, high-
  confidence example of the mechanism and long lead time.

## Case 3 — EO 14028 → OMB zero-trust mandate → federal EDR / ICAM / zero-trust procurement
- **Chain:** **EO 14028 (May 12, 2021)** mandates EDR, zero trust, SBOM → **OMB M-22-09 (Jan 26, 2022)**
  sets FY24 zero-trust targets → agencies (incl. defense components) procure EDR, ICAM, and zero-trust
  integration services; CISA CDM task orders expand.
- **Cutoff:** **May 12, 2021** (EO), sharpened at **Jan 26, 2022** (OMB memo).
- **Knowable then (high conf):** the EO explicitly directed specific capabilities with deadlines across
  federal agencies — a procurement wave in EDR/ICAM/zero-trust was directed, not speculative.
- **Hindsight-only:** which specific task orders/vehicles, exact timing per agency, winners.
- **Pyrnova inference at cutoff:** "Expect EDR/ICAM/zero-trust services demand across FY22–FY24; CDM and
  agency-specific vehicles are the channels; the OMB memo is the timing accelerant." Cyber services
  contractors could align capability and past performance well ahead of task orders.
- **Falsifiers at the time:** unfunded-mandate risk; slow agency adoption; budget continuing-resolution
  stalls; capability defined but money not appropriated.
- **Observable outcome:** EDR deployments (CISA-funded), ICAM/zero-trust task orders 2022–2024.
- **Lead time:** EO → visible procurement ≈ **9–18 months**; OMB memo tightened the window.
- **Beachhead relevance (high):** defense components are in scope; cyber is a common capability for the
  target archetype — closest of the three to the Capture Radar buyer.

## What this casebook proves — and does not
- **Proves:** upstream statute/EO/policy reliably precedes procurement by a knowable lead time, and the
  *pre-solicitation tell* (RFI / NOFO / OMB memo) can be tracked before the RFP. This is the
  differentiated claim.
- **Does not prove:** that Pyrnova specifically predicted any of these (it did not — this is
  retrospective). Live forward proof (the prediction log) is the asset that will.
- **Method discipline demonstrated:** each case fixes a cutoff, separates knowable-then from
  hindsight-only, states the inference a disciplined analyst could have made, and lists the falsifiers
  that were live at the time.
