## Major conclusions

1. **The long-term thesis survives.** Pyrnova's ambition — a continuously updated, historically
   auditable model of what external change means economically to a specific organization — remains
   sound as long-term architectural direction.
2. **The launch thesis narrows.** Breadth of data and coverage is *not* the differentiation. The launch
   must be a narrow, finishable wedge.
3. **Breadth is not differentiation.** Broad data aggregation, generic company research, generic AI
   search, a knowledge graph by itself, dashboards, or alerts by themselves do not differentiate
   Pyrnova.
4. **The initial wedge is US federal contractors.** Specifically mid-market federal/defense contractors
   with lean BD/capture/strategy capacity and insufficient analyst bandwidth for continuous monitoring.
5. **The differentiated product is the material-change → consequence → customer-relevance loop.**
   Determine what materially changed, why it affects *this* organization, what evidence supports the
   conclusion, what remains uncertain, what merits investigation, and what eventually happened.
6. **Dossiers, search, and AI are supporting capabilities, not the moat.**
7. **Finishability is the greatest founder-controlled risk.** Scope expansion — not competition or
   technology — is the most likely cause of failure.
8. **Evidence independence matters.** Conclusions must be defensible from retained, attributable
   evidence, not from a single flattened source of truth.
9. **Data rights can become existential.** Premium/licensed corpora carry rights obligations that must
   be isolated so they cannot contaminate rights-safe intelligence.
10. **Supplier/capacity claims require conservative treatment.** Replacement-supplier and capacity
    claims must not be asserted without qualification evidence.
11. **Global real-time intelligence should be deferred.** A Dataminr-equivalent global detection layer
    is not economically sensible at launch.
12. **Expansion should be customer-pulled.** New capability is pulled by demonstrated user value, not
    pushed by architectural possibility.
13. **Historical / outcome intelligence is potentially highly defensible.** "What could have been known
    then?", customer-specific exposure/outcome history, and longitudinal calibration are candidate
    durable moats.
14. **Phase 1 requires falsifiable commercial validation.** The wedge must be validated against real
    customer decision value, not internal conviction.

## Resulting decisions

- D-044 — Long-term thesis recorded as architectural direction (not Phase 1 scope).
- D-045 — Phase 1 wedge locked to US federal contractors (≈$50M–$300M revenue; defense/industrial/
  technology/engineering/infrastructure).
- D-046 — Material-change/consequence/customer-relevance loop is the Phase 1 differentiated product;
  Material Changes is the dominant UX; dossier/search/graph are supporting surfaces.
- D-047 — Finishability rule (binding): a missing capability may block a phase only for
  correctness/safety/architectural-integrity/existing-acceptance/real-customer-value reasons.
- D-048 — Phase 1 explicit non-goals recorded as durable and deferred/customer-gated.
- D-049 — Defensibility / moat authority (ranked candidate moats; explicit non-moats).
- D-050 — Research→decision traceability established; this review indexed and linked.
